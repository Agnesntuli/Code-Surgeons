Bursaries and Bursary loans

Want to learn a thing or two about Bursaries and Bursary loans, well rest assured because here in this section, you will then know even the smallest details regardless of if you were to be experienced or as a beginner based on how to interact with BURSARIES NOR BURSARY LOANS


NOW WHAT IS A BURSARY

Now a Bursary is a non-repayable grant or financial award given to students to fund their studies. It is typically awarded based financial need, academic merit, or some specifics, often in demand fields of study.

Repayment: Usually non-repayable, means you won’t need to pay back the money after graduating.

Conditions: Some certain bursaries may include “Conditions”, meaning many corporate bursaries require a “Work-back” an agreement, where you agree to work for the company for a set period (e.g., one year of work for every year of funding).


Maintenance: Bursaries often need to maintain a certain academic average to keep the funding, meaning you should work hard on your studies to keep a stable grade that can be funded.

Coverage: Can be partial (as in paying fir tuitions only) or full (as in paying for tuition, accommodation, meals, and books)


What is a Bursary Loan?

Repayment: Must be paid, usually starting after you graduate and star working.

Interest: Accrues interest, increasing the total amount you pay back.

Requirements: Usually require a co-signer (e.g., Parent or Guardian) with good credit to stand surety.


Major Funding Sources in South Africa

NSFAS (National Student Financial Aid Scheme:

Provides funding to students with a combined household income of less than R350,000 per annum. This is now a grant-based system, meaning it does not need to be repaid if you pass.

Corporated Bursaries: Companies like Sasol, Eskom, Transnet, and Bank offer bursaries, often in specialized fields to secure future employees.

University Financial Aid: Offices at universities provide internal bursaries and scholarships based on merit or need.

Bank Student Loan: Commercial banks (e.g., FNB, Standard Bank, Absa, Nedbank) offer loans, typically to cover tuition and study materials.


Tips for Applying

Apply Early: Many Bursaries close 12 months in advance of students, often between August and December.

Check requirements: Ensure your field of study matches the bursary focus (e.g., Funza Lushaka for Education)

Prepare Documents: Have certified copies of your ID, Matric results, and parents proof of income ready.

Draft a Strong Motivational Letter: Clearly explain why you need or deserve the Funding.

Never pay to Apply: Legitimate Bursaries do not charge application fees.


What to do if you Fail?

Bursary: You may lose funding or be required to pay back some of the funds if you violate the agreement.

Loan: The debt still remains and must still be repaid.

Well now, what are Scholarships?

Well Scholarships are/is: The first step towards reducing student debt. Scholarships are financial gifts awarded to students based on merit, need, or specific criteria (such as talent, community involvement, or field of study) that do not need to be repaid.


What are the key types of Scholarships?

Merit-Based: Awarded for academic achievements, high grades, or stella test scores.

Need-Based: Awarded based on financial need of the student or their families.

Talent/Activity-Based: Awarded for extracurricular activities like sports, music, or leadership (e.g., Kovsie Sport Bursary).

Niche Scholarships: Targeted toward specific demographics, fields of study (e.g., STEM), or unique backgrounds.

Corporated/ Private Scholarships: Offered by companies or organizations (e.g., Old Mutual, Amazon, etc.).


Where to start looking for Scholarships?

University Financial Aid: This is the best place to start, as universities often offer scholarships specifically for their incoming and existing students.

Online Databases: Use sites like Fastweb, Scholarships.com, and Cappex to filter opportunities.

Local Community: Look for Scholarships from local businesses, foundations, and community organizations, as these often have fewer applicants.


Key tips for Beginner Applicants

Start early: Being your search and application process at least a year in advance if possible.

Stay organized: Create a calendar to track application deadlines, as missing one could result in disqualification.

Read Instructions Carefully: Ensure you fulfill all requirements (e.g., Letters of recommendation, transcripts) to avoid unnecessary disqualifications.

Apply for many: Do not limit yourself to only one or two applications, as you can apply for multiple Scholarships simultaneously.


Common misconceptions

“Only top students get scholarships.”: Well, that is a FALSE statement. Many scholarships are based on community involvement, unique talents, or financial needs.

“Scholarships only cover tuition.”: Also, a FALSE statement. Some scholarships can cover textbooks, accommodation, and living costs.

“Cannot apply too many.”: FALSE. You are encouraged to apply to many scholarships to increase your chance of winning.


Essential Steps for a Winning Application

Write a compelling essay: Share your personal story, goals, and why you deserve the award, ensuring that it is authentic and well-written.

Can speak to your character and achievements.

Proofread: Check all documents for errors before submission.

University/Collage Applications for Beginners

1 Research and Preparations (Before You Apply)

Explore Options: Look at prospectuses, attend university open days, and use university websites to identify suitable courses.

Check Requirements: Ensure you meet the minimum academic standards (e.g., Admission Point Score- APS) for your chosen courses.

Identify Deadlines: summarize ways to create notes for closing dates and students’ accommodation.

Start to Apply Early: Applying early increases your chances, especially for competitive courses like Engineering or Nursing, which fill up fast.


2 Gather Necessary Documents

Most Universities need Certified copies of the following documents to be uploaded:

ID Document: A certified copy of your ID.

School Results: Final Grade 11 results (for current matric) or grade 12 Certificate (If already passed).

Parent/Guardian concern: if you are under the age of 18.


3 Appliction Process

Online Application: Visit the specific University/Collage website and follow the “Apply” link.

Fill Details: Complete all sections carefully. Double-check everything, as an incomplete application can lead to rejections.

Pay application fee: Some institutions, like UJ and UFS, offer free online applications while others charge a fee (e.g., R 150 for Unisa).

Submit: Submit your application and keep record of all documents and the confirmation email.


4 Key tips for Beginners

Apply early: Do not for final matric results to apply, spots are limited and fill up quickly.

Check Eligibility: Use APS calculations on the University websites to confirm you qualify for your chosen course.

Check Status: Periodically log into the student portal to track your application status (e.g., UJ, UP, Wits).

Beware of Scams: Universities do not ask for money to require space.

Understand Terms: If you receive conditional admission, your final acceptance depends on your final NSC results.
